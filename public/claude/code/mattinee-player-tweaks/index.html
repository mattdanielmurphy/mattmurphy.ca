Mattinee player tweaks

Small tweaks to the string player in e.html, after the long design thread.

17:30 Middle of the screen and the leaf cursor

You asked for click to pause and drag to seek in the middle of the screen, then a custom cursor with default, pointer and loading states. Both are in e.html. The Vercel deploy failed, so the public link still shows the old version.

Deploy blocked

Vercel refused the deploy: the account has used its 100 free deployments for the day ("try again in 24 hours"). The new version runs at http://pages.test/mattinee-playful/e.html?v=moss&screen=paused&theme=dark on your Mac. The public link stays on the old version until a deploy goes through. I did not move the page to another host.

The middle mark

In the middle of the screen, the cursor becomes a little mark. It shows a pause icon while playing and a play icon while paused, so it always shows what a click will do. The zone runs from 120 px below the top to 190 px above the bottom, which keeps it clear of the buttons.

  • The outline re-rolls each time it appears, like the other shapes.
  • Two small arrows beside the mark hint that you can drag. I added those because every gesture needs a visible hint. They are one line to remove.
  • It fades after 1.5 seconds without movement, so the film stays clean.

Clicking the video did nothing before, because a layer sat on top of it. That is fixed.

Drag to seek

A press becomes a drag after 10 px sideways. Below that, it counts as a click.

  • The mark opens into a pill showing the change and the target time, such as +2:23 and 21:05, with arrows in the direction you are going.
  • The string comes up and the preview frame shows, so you see the dot move.
  • The video seeks when you let go. Esc while dragging cancels and returns to where you started.

How far a drag seeks: 0.25 seconds per px at first, then faster, so half a screen covers half the video. Small moves stay fine, and a long film is still reachable. A drag of 400 px on this 29-minute episode moves about 3.5 minutes, and on a 2-hour film about 11 minutes.

Rejected: a fixed seconds-per-px rate, which is either too slow for films or too twitchy for short moves.

The leaf cursor

The cursor is a leaf from the saplings, drawn on the page, and it shows on every screen.

  • Default: one leaf. The tip is the exact click point.
  • Pointer: over anything clickable, the leaf grows a little, changes colour and sprouts two more leaves.
  • Loading: a three-strand loop spins round the leaf. It shows on the buffering screen and for 0.9 seconds after a seek.
  • Press: the leaf dips so every click visibly lands.

Colours use existing Moss roles only, in both themes. The middle mark counts as the cursor in that zone, except while loading, when the leaf with its loop takes over.

Why drawn instead of the system cursor: the system cursor cannot spring, sprout or spin smoothly, and this keeps one shared look. The cost is that it can trail the real mouse by a frame.

Your message ended on a comma after "loading", so there may have been more states.

Touch and the remote

These are calls I made, since you did not specify them.

  • Touch: a finger has no hover, so the first tap brings up the controls and a second tap while they are up pauses. Dragging seeks, with the pill floating above the finger. Nothing tested this on a real iPad.
  • Streamer remote: nothing changes. There is no cursor, so Centre still plays and Left or Right still scrub.

Tested

Headless Chrome, 1920×1080, on the local page, in light and dark, with no page errors:

  • hover, click to pause and play, drag both ways, Esc cancel, release to seek
  • mark fading when idle, leaf returning outside the zone
  • pointer on buttons and posters, loading on the buffering screen

Untested: a real mouse feel, a real iPad, and the live public page. The cursor trail is unknown until you try it.

For wiring into the apps

All values live in PLAYER at the top of the script. MIDDLE and CURSOR hold no video state: the player hands MIDDLE its getters and callbacks, and the app sets loading with CURSOR.loading(true). The fake wait after a seek is in the demo wiring only. To undo, remove rounds 13 and 14 in the CSS and the two modules in the script.

The Mac app has no web view, so the leaf there will need native cursor images. The loading spin would mean swapping images.

18:10 Volume drag and a paused tag

You asked for up and down drags to set the volume, the same way as seeking. You also said the paused mark felt wrong: it looked like a button, and the cursor showed play on top of it while it showed pause. Both are done, on the local page only, because Vercel is still refusing deploys.

Volume drag

A drag locks to its first direction, so sideways seeks and up or down sets the volume. It can't switch partway through.

  • The mark opens into a tall pill with the percentage, a meter wound like the string, and a speaker that becomes a muted speaker at 0%.
  • 300 px goes from silent to full. Volume changes live as you drag, because you need to hear it. Esc puts it back.
  • The pill stays where you pressed and only the meter moves. On release it fades there, and the mark returns at your cursor on the next move.
  • The mark now has four small arrows, so up and down are hinted too.

This page has no sound, so the volume is a stand-in number here. Touch gets the same drag, with the pill above the finger.

Paused tag

The real problem was two icons saying opposite things. The fix is to keep one icon, which is the action under your cursor, and make the state a word.

  • "Paused" now hangs on a thread from the dot, and swings briefly when you pause. It is not a button: it has no icon, ignores the cursor and sits below the string. It also keeps the middle of the frame clear, which helps when you pause to look at something.
  • I removed the big pause mark and its hover squish.
  • The alternative is the word in the middle of the screen: add &ind=word to the URL.

Smaller fix

The mark and pills were stretched from one shape, which gave them spikes and notches. Each is now drawn at its real size, so the outlines are calmer.

Tested

Headless Chrome on the local page, in light and dark, with no page errors: volume up, down to mute, Esc, release, seek after volume, pause tag, and the earlier seek and cursor checks.

Untested: real mouse feel, a real iPad, real audio, and the public page.

18:40 Calmer poster hover

You wanted the poster hover dialled back a little, with its character kept. The change is on the local page only, because Vercel is still refusing deploys.

What changed

All four are named settings under PLAYER.poster, where 1 is the old strength.

  • Stretch 0.75: the poster grows and squashes 25% less.
  • Lean 0.7: it tilts and shears 30% less.
  • Shape 0.7: the outline changes 30% less.
  • Settle 1.25: the bounce dies away 25% faster.

Measured over 20 hovers, peak stretch went from about 7.6% to about 5%. These numbers vary a little because each hover is random. Buttons, the selected poster and the cursor are unchanged.

Tested

Headless Chrome on the local Home page, in light and dark, with no page errors. Untested: how it feels with a real mouse.

19:15 Hover targets, poster feel and what the designs leave out

You and the coordinator raised three things: hover targets moving away from a still cursor, the poster hover running too long and feeling jerky on quick moves, and player features missing from the designs.

Hover targets

The hit areas from round 7 were still, but the moving layers inside them still took the mouse. A poster's stretched edge counted as hover, so hover ended when that edge shrank back, with no cursor movement. The same thing could happen against a neighbour's stretched edge. Round 7 only moved the animation inward; it never turned the mouse off for that layer.

  • The moving layer now takes no pointer events, everywhere.
  • Press and selected effects used to scale the hit area itself on buttons and posters. They now run on the inner layer.
  • String buttons have a wider still hit area, because their label opens past the icon. Otherwise moving onto the label would close it.

One small loss: the progress bar under a poster no longer has its own hover wobble, since it sits on the poster's moving layer.

Poster feel

The bounce is shorter, and the snap on quick moves is gone.

  • length .55 makes the bounce last about half as long. Measured time went from about 600 ms to about 360 ms.
  • Every bounce used to start already offset, which made one sudden frame on every hover. It now swells in over 0.12 seconds (ease).
  • A new hover used to cancel the running bounce, which jumped the poster back. Bounces now carry on, and landing again early just lets the current one finish.
  • Leaving used to fast-forward the bounce at 7 times speed. Posters now use 1.8 (leave), and the outline returns on a soft spring (backStiff, backDamp).
  • Outline points keep their speed when the target changes, so a change of direction bends the motion instead of kicking it.

Flicking on, off and on again, the biggest single-frame jump fell from about 9% to about 1%.

The soft start, no-cancel and outline changes apply to every bouncing element, because they were faults everywhere. Length and leave speed are poster-only.

What the designs leave out

Compared with what the Mac, iPad and Streamer players do today.

Your three:

  • X-ray: cards beside faces on pause, "Actors seen so far", next person, more, hide, and IMDb and Wikipedia links.
  • What did they say?: the recap card of recent lines.
  • Subtitles: both the settings panel (track, type, size) and how the captions themselves look on the video.

Also missing:

  • Panels behind existing buttons: Episodes and Source are on the string but open nothing. Source needs the list of copies, with the one playing marked.
  • Audio: track choice, plus audio delay on iPad.
  • Speed: the Streamer has a speed picker.
  • Slow-connection card: the meter exists, but not the choices "Play now, it may stop", "Let Mattinee choose" and "Go back".
  • Songs: "Songs so far" and "Playing now", with Open in Music, from the Mac overlay.
  • Next and Previous episode, and what happens at the credits.
  • Skip feedback: the +10 and -10 flashes, and the hold-to-scan state on the remote.
  • Smaller states: mute, full screen, iPad's close button and skip buttons, and playback errors.

The string has only four slots, so most of these need one shared panel pattern rather than separate screens. Open design like this suits your own thinking first, so it stays unanchored.

Tested

Headless Chrome on the local page. A cursor parked for 1.2 seconds on each of 8 edge and corner spots of 11 elements, once just inside and once just outside: 176 parks on the home, detail and buffering setup plus the player rerun. No hover flipped in any park on the new version. On the old version the same test caught 2 flips, one on a detail button and one on a player button, so it does reproduce the fault, though rarely. Parks only caught the case that happened within 1.2 seconds.

Rounded corners sit outside their rounded shape, so hover there simply never starts. That is stable, not a flicker.

All the earlier seek, volume, pause and cursor checks still pass in light and dark with no page errors.

Untested: a real mouse. The automated cursor can't prove there's no flicker in your hands, so park your real cursor on a few poster edges.

20:00 Filling the player's design gaps

You asked me to fill every gap in the existing style, connected and organic. All of it is now in e.html, built as one system, on the local page only because Vercel is still refusing deploys. This chose one direction instead of three, because you said to just extend the style.

The system

Everything hangs off the string: one organic panel that grows up from its bead on two threads, sits a little crooked, and re-rolls its outline each time it opens. PLAYER.panel holds its settings.

What was added

  • Episodes: season and five stills. The current one is marked. Previous is here too.
  • Subtitles panel: Subtitles, Show, Size, Audio, Delay and Speed, all in one panel, because the string has no spare slots.
  • Captions: each line sits on its own torn scrap, with a new outline per line, and follows the size setting.
  • Source: the copies, with the one playing marked, plus Let Mattinee choose.
  • What did they say?: a new small bead right of the timeline, also the W key.
  • Next episode: a new small bead left of the timeline, also the N key. Up next opens by itself near the end, with a string that unwinds for 8 seconds.
  • X-ray: shows after a pause. Looking for faces, then a brush ring round the face tied by a thread to the card, with IMDb, Wikipedia, More and Hide. Actors seen so far sits on the left. The empty state is &xray=none.
  • Songs: Playing now and Songs so far, beside X-ray, because that is when the Mac shows them.
  • Slow connection and failure: a card beside the wait meter on the buffering screen. It arrives after 4 seconds, or use &gate=slow or &gate=error.
  • Skip feedback: a +10 flash on the side you skipped, adding up as you keep pressing.
  • Muted: a tag hanging under the total time, plus a flash. The M key.
  • Full screen: a small bead at the top right, also F.

Decisions

The two small beads sit in the gaps either side of the timeline, so your tuned string layout did not move. Rejected: a third full button each side, which would have changed your spacing.

You said the panel buttons were a little too crazy, so they are now rounded pills with a slight wobble (PLAYER.panel.button), not the big blobs from the main buttons. On hover they move only a little.

Limits

Lines, songs, copies and episodes are stand-ins. X-ray shows one person, so there is no Next person yet, and the mute and speed choices change nothing real because this page has no video. Panels are built once at load. When wired to the apps, a new list of copies or episodes will need its outlines redrawn.

Tested

Headless Chrome on the local page, light and dark, with no page errors: every panel opened, X-ray, flash and the gate card, and the earlier seek, volume, cursor and parked-hover checks. Untested with a real mouse or remote.

20:40 One bead for subtitles and What did they say?

You didn't want subtitles over the controls, you wanted the Next button gone, and you wanted one button to do both subtitle jobs. All three are done.

How the bead works

  • At rest it is the Subtitles bead, dimmed when subtitles are off.
  • On hover it morphs into a "What did they say?" pill. Pressing it opens that panel.
  • Above it a sprout grows on two stems with little leaves: a Subtitles on or off switch on the left, and a gear on the right.
  • The gear opens the settings panel.
  • Crossing the gap: the sprout's still hit area touches the bead's, and it lingers 280 ms after you leave, so moving up to it never closes it.
  • While a panel is open the sprout tucks away, and it returns when the panel closes if you are still there.
  • Touch: the first tap grows the sprout and the second asks What did they say?
  • Remote: picking the bead morphs it, Up goes into the sprout, Left and Right choose, Down comes back.

The stems and outlines re-roll each time. S also flips subtitles, and W still asks.

Decisions

The settings panel keeps Audio, Delay and Speed as well as subtitle track, type and size, because they have nowhere else to live. "Off" left the track list since the switch does that job now.

Next episode is gone as a button. Up next still opens by itself near the end, and the Episodes panel covers picking another one. The N key is gone too.

Captions now sit 100 px to 136 px above the bottom, following the controls as they grow, so they clear them at every size. Measured gap is 27 px or more.

The morphed pill slightly overlaps the time pill while hovered.

Tested

Headless Chrome, light and dark, with no page errors: rest, hover, switch, gear, Esc, press, leaving. A real mouse is still untested.
