00:50 Four directions and a shared system

You asked for a playful, round, high-contrast look, based on the earlier mockups, unified across devices. No app code changed.

Restored base

Figma access was declined, so the base is the exported renders in the mockup viewer. Same layouts, same content.

My recommendation

Jelly Bean. It is calm over video, readable from the sofa and simple to build natively. Rejected: Cut Paper (its player band hides more film), Crayon Line (fine lines, hard natively), Sticker Book (likely to tire in daily use).

Assumptions and risks

The buffer meter was designed without seeing the buffering thread's final version: centred, fills as video saves, plays by itself. Hues come from candidate palettes, because the master palette is not picked. Fredoka is a fairly common font, so a final pick might swap it. Streamer dark default is my proposed exception. Only tested on rendered pages, not on the Streamer. "18 min left" was corrected to 10 to match 18:42 of 29:10.

01:30 Round 2: Jelly Bean, grown up

You chose Jelly Bean but wanted it less childish because your parents use it, with some edge, artistic flair and comfort kept. You also wanted smoother, rounder shapes with no jagged lines.

What changed

Removed tilts, glossy highlights, blobs and wavy edges. Corners are smooth and only slightly uneven. Added the Sticker Book timeline, with its thick band and large dot, and one soft bounce on hover. Built three colour moods and four calmer meters.

Recommendation

Velvet with the Ring meter. Velvet is warmest and has the clearest personality; the Ring shows seconds plainly. Rejected as first choice: Spruce (cooler), Dusk (closest to round 1, least change), Pour and Hills (no number inside the shape).

Risks

Hover bounce is declared in the page, but I did not watch it play. Light-mode lime dot relies on its dark ring for contrast. Colours still come from candidate palettes. All of this is untested on the Streamer.

02:10 Round 3: unique shapes and the Please wait meter

You said round 2 was too restrained, so colour came back and every shape got its own character.

What changed in round 3

Buttons, posters, the tray, the timeline band and the meter are each generated as a smooth lopsided curve, so no two match and none can get jagged. Focus has a lime shape sitting off-register behind it. Rooms now carry more colour. The meter mixes Hills and Pour, says Please wait above it, and shows a big seconds counter in the middle with no text below. It loops from empty to full.

Notes for the build

Unique shapes need a real drawing step in Compose and SwiftUI, which is more work than plain rounded corners. The demo loop runs faster than real time so you can see all of it. Not yet tried on the Streamer.

02:50 Round 4: organic

You found a jagged corner, wanted more character and an organic feel, and found the palettes too feminine or corporate.

What changed in round 4

The shape generator now spaces its points evenly, which removes the corner kink. Shapes are lumpier and more alive. Colours are Moss, Reef and Night Swim. Titles and buttons use Caprasimo, a chunky soft face. Selection is either an askew two-colour blob or a loose brush stroke behind the poster. Home and detail get a large soft hill behind them.

Why Moss and blob

Moss feels the most natural and least like a brand. The blob is clearest from the sofa, while the brush has more character but is thinner.

03:20 Round 5: calmer big shapes and score bars

You asked for more padding, cohesive big shapes, a smaller selection blob, and a clearer score.

What changed in round 5

The player tray has more inner padding and only slow, gentle curves. Hero art and the timeline band use the same calm shapes. Small buttons stay lumpy. The selection blob is much tighter. Scores are four rising green bars. Detail also shows the number in a plain sans font.

Score bars rule, my guess

Four bars at 8 or higher, three at 7, two at 6, one below that. Check this once real scores are on screen.

03:45 Random squish for every element

You asked for each button to have its own squish style, with a new random variation on every landing, across the whole interface.

How the squish works

Each element gets a fixed character from its name: stretch direction, bounce speed, settling time, lean and pivot point. Every hover, press or focus rolls new values inside that character, then a spring turns them into a one-off animation. Applied to buttons, posters, timeline and dot, pause mark, Please wait, headings, times and score bars. It copies easily to Compose and SwiftUI.

Limits

It moves and squashes shapes, but the outlines themselves do not reshape. Confirmed by sampling the animations: the same button differs each time, and two buttons differ from each other. How it feels needs your eye. Remote focus on the Streamer is untested.

04:30 Round 6: folding player and generated shapes

You asked for less of the picture covered, a folding UI, fewer options, a scrubbing view, looser bounce, and truly generated buttons. You also said one-off instructions should not become rules.

The new player

Title sits at the very top. One bottom line holds Episodes, Subtitles, Source and Home with icons, then the timeline. Speed, Audio, Previous and Next are gone. After 3.5 seconds it folds to a sliver with tiny icon nubs and a thin timeline; nothing vanishes. On the timeline or with Left and Right, buttons shrink to nubs, the timeline thickens and a preview frame with the time appears.

Generated shapes and bounce

Each landing on a button or poster draws a new random outline and the real curve morphs into it on a spring, then snaps back on leave. Bounces are slower and looser, with five moods (squash, lean, shear, double bounce, wide stretch) that never repeat twice in a row. Leaving plays the rest of the bounce seven times faster.

Decisions and open points

Removed the saved squish rule. Tray fold timing is 3.5 seconds, my guess. The Source icon is a placeholder drawing. The Episodes picker and Source list screens are not designed yet. Remote behaviour on the Streamer is untested. Flat countdown edges fixed, posters too.

05:10 Round 7: steady hit areas and icon buttons

You loved the generated buttons, but found hover could run away from the mouse, the bounce a bit long, and the timeline expansion wrong.

What changed in round 7

Every interactive element now has a still invisible hit area while a separate inner layer does the moving. Bounces run about 0.65 to 1.5 seconds. Buttons rest as icons and open to show text on hover or selection, inside a reserved space so the timeline never shifts. Hovering the timeline only previews a frame. Pressing or dragging seeks. The sliders page saves your values in that browser.

Open points

The mouse mode drops the initial selection on first move. Remote focus on the Streamer is untested. Labels may push the next button a little as they open.

05:40 Round 8: symmetric bar and loud selection

You asked for symmetry, a centred timeline, no weird gap, more margin, and stronger selection.

What changed in round 8

Home and Episodes sit left because they leave this episode. Subtitles and Source sit right because they act on it. The timeline is exactly centred. Each button has a fixed 172 px slot, so a label opens in place and neighbours do not move. Selection is a bright fill with a large contrasting halo, and it stays until moved. In the mock, Down picks a button, Left and Right move it, Up clears it.

06:10 Round 9: three pills or one bar

You asked for tighter icons, a three-pill version, and a smaller progress bar.

What changed in round 9

The centre block is fixed on screen and the button groups grow outward, so the timeline never moves. Icons now sit 8 px apart. Opening a label does push the outer button of that group, which is the cost of tight spacing. The page compares three pills with one bar. The bar is about 30 percent thinner and about 35 percent shorter, and the dot shrank to match.

Notes

"Size" could mean thickness or length, so I did both. Your y-axis scrubber idea is in the project ideas file for later. I did not save a rule for this round.

06:50 Round 10: the bar meets the cursor

You said the margin had gone too far, that expanded buttons felt slippery, and you wanted the bar to grow as the cursor approaches.

What changed in round 10

The bar has one continuous size tied to cursor height. It starts growing at the lower 30 percent and is full size at the lower 15 percent, ahead of the cursor. The title grows the same way near the top. Icon gaps rose from 8 to 32 px at full size. Labels now open outward, so the icon you aim for keeps its place while another label closes, and closing waits 0.12 seconds. In the live page, the pause mark now shows only when paused, and pausing holds the bar open.

Open points

The Streamer has no cursor, so there the remote opens the bar and it folds after a pause. I checked the sizes at each cursor height, and only your hand can judge the slip.

07:20 Round 11: height only, and does this translate?

You said the bar slid sideways as it grew, wanted it to finish sooner, and asked for gaps that open when switching buttons. You also asked whether this code will really translate.

What changed in round 11

Horizontal positions no longer change with cursor height; only height and drawn size do. Growth starts at the middle of the screen and finishes at 67 percent down on a sharper curve. Switching from an open button to its neighbour opens the gap by the closing label's width.

What translates

The Mac app is a window around server pages, so this page's approach carries over directly. iPad needs SwiftUI rebuilds, and touch loses hover and proximity. The Streamer needs Compose rebuilds with remote focus, and has no cursor at all. Shapes, spring numbers, palette and layout decisions carry everywhere. Performance of shape morphing on the Streamer is unproven, so test one button there before building the rest.

Web UI everywhere

Yes, this is achievable: thin apps on each device show one set of web pages, with the native video player underneath a transparent UI. The Mac app already works this way. Tested with a real cursor: switching from Episodes to Home moved the Home icon by under 10 px. Main risk is WebView speed on the Streamer, so test this mock there first. Remote focus must be handled in the web layer.
