Why ActivityWatch. A coach needs to know "was Logic open, or Glass TV?", and window titles answer that. Screen recording answers far more than that, at a real cost in battery and privacy. Your Mac also has a history of freezes, which makes the heavy tools a worse bet.

Agents can skip MCP. A small script reading ActivityWatch's local API can write a one-page daily summary. That costs no tokens until an agent reads it, and it avoids a third-party connector.

Permissions. ActivityWatch's own pages ask for Accessibility, not Screen Recording. Those pages are old, so I can't promise newer macOS won't ask for more.

What's left of your old tools. Pieces left only a launch file that points at an app that's gone, and it does nothing. ScreenMemory is installed but has never run and holds no data. I didn't check whether agents could read it. Apple's Screen Time data is locked without Full Disk Access, and I didn't try to get around that.

How coordinators work

Each one is a small job that wakes, reads its state from disk, does one thing and stops. It isn't a long chat. Your Oct 31 report found coordinators use about a third of your model usage, so a long chat per life area would be the wrong shape.

The rules are the same for all of them:

  • The step is small enough for a bad day.
  • A miss shrinks the next step, with no guilt.
  • At most one message a day, only when there's something to do right now.
  • Two ignored nudges stop the nudging, and three skips in a row pause it.
  • One new coordinator at a time, after the last has held for a week.

Full conventions are in study-coach.md.

Facts instead of fear

I'd skip the scare tactics. Dread is usually what feeds procrastination, and fear has already been part of how Logic feels. So the page shows only honest facts: the countdown at the top, and how much of the map is solved. It never comments on them.

Your Claude habit is the positive lever you were looking for. Finishing a bite is what unlocks Claude, and your gate already does that.

The game

  • Right answer: Correct shows for 1.5 seconds and moves on by itself, through a few seconds of a minigame.
  • Three minigames: stop the marker on the green, pop balloons, and tap rings on the beat. The last one is for the drummer in you.
  • Streaks: consecutive right answers multiply your points up to ×5, and the games get a little harder as they climb.
  • Wrong answer: you lose only the streak. It shows the explanation, and the problem comes back a few cards later.
  • Score: it carries across days, and each bite shows whether you beat your best.

You can skip any game with one tap. Your knobs are autoNextMs, balloonMs and ringBeatMs in the coach settings.
