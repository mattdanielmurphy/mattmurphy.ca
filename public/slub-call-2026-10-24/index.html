Slub call: Matt & Tyler, Oct 24, 2026

About 3.5 hours. Tyler presented the Slub deck, then the two talked about how to work together, and Matt demoed his explainer reels.

The pitch, in short

  • Thesis: "We're not creative developers, we're developers of creativity." AI is about to make utility apps nearly free: cheap clones everywhere, the $9.99 app model gone, and even pricey tools like Ableton and UAD plugins devalued. What stays valuable is taste, brand and the human creative process.
  • Why a prompt can't make magic: language is ambiguous, intent is discovered along the way, and the real choices only show up once you meet them. Magic is earned one human decision at a time.
  • Slub's answer: be the brand, "the sweater with soul". Any factory can make a sweater. Slub is the human irregularity that makes it beautiful.
  • Debate: Matt pushed back that developers change shape rather than disappear, that cloning is real but not instant, and that most clones will be slop without human judgment. Tyler took the refinements, and Matt agreed with about 95% of the deck.

Decisions

  • Say "free", not "open source". The apps are free to use. The pathing and prompt-gate know-how stays closed.
  • Business model: a brand partnership with an AI company, ideally Anthropic. "They bring the reach and machinery, we bring the taste." The AI company plays the record label and Slub the artist. The goal is pitch meetings within about six months.
  • Roadmap: follow The Lean Startup. Iterate fast, skip waiting for a perfect launch, check assumptions early, and get a first beta to about 10 people they know.
  • Roles: Tyler leads concept and direction, Matt leads building (roughly 70/30, complementary), and Kristy (Tyler's girlfriend) handles marketing (target: 50k real followers).

Best ideas from the call

  1. Prompt gates. Building becomes a guided chain of micro-decisions, so a human stays in the creator's seat. Each fork offers 2 to 4 options with no value judgment. Example: "tighten this up" becomes a quantize slider instead of a destructive edit.
  2. Fork the journey, not the code. If someone has already built something similar, you walk their decision path, branch off where yours differs, and keep "ghost lines" to other branches so problems already solved there come back up.
  3. Read the user's level without asking. Words used and apps installed set how long the path is. Start with a short questionnaire whose first question is "Will you use this once?". A yes skips the rest.
  4. Wireframe auditions. Show a cheap wireframe (background, left pane, right pane) or a 30-second live preview instead of eight polished mockups, and go with the gut reaction.
  5. Personal taste profile. The agent learns each person's taste, so its first try lands.
  6. Grow a forest, not a tree. Run a postmortem on every app: strip its labels and see what it really enables. For example, the trackpad segmentation from the mixer could work in any app. Small projects are seeds that can crossbreed.
  7. Feel-first controls. Instead of labelling a knob, evoke the feeling it gives (filter cutoff = surfacing from underwater), and group parameters so people can follow the vibe.
  8. Eye-tracked ghost knob. A control appears on whatever you're looking at.
  9. One Slub hub. Shared architecture, themes and file locations, plus checks that key commands don't clash across apps.
  10. Live tweak panels. Sliders for every setting in any app beat describing a change to an AI.
  11. Call highlights. Flag transcript moments where volume or laughter spikes.

Working together

  • Raise concerns right away instead of letting them fester.
  • When the two disagree, the louder one doesn't win. Test both options and aim for a hybrid both agree with.
  • Agreed times are firm, with buffers between commitments.
  • Meetings get a set agenda, and conceptual work happens in blocks (e.g. switch after about 3 hours of building).
  • Spend about a week aligning the two Claude setups: compare rules, keep the best of both, maybe share them on GitHub or move to a shared business account.

Reel demo feedback

  • Captions: they fight the visuals, because you can't read and watch at the same time. Add a way to turn them fully off.
  • Voice: the default is too slow.
  • Scroll reader: the highlight works well as a focus bubble. It needs a bigger skip threshold, a jump after lingering about a second on a word, no delay before reading starts, and clearer names for the tweak knobs ("fly speed").
